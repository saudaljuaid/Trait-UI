openrfs-mark-512.png is that repository's 512-pixel build of it, kept
for reference. Nothing uses it, and the reason is worth writing down:
the build lifts the drawing's white to transparency, and the fish's eyes
are white, so in the trimmed mark the eyes are HOLES. It is invisible on
a white page and it is not invisible anywhere else.

openrfs-mark-flat.png is what the ASCII was made from: that mark
composited over white with a 20px margin and doubled. Over white the
lifted whites come back, so it is the drawing with a perfectly clean
ground and none of the JPEG's speckle. sha256

openrfs-logo.txt is the ASCII, and it is NOT this project's own work:

  Tool:     https://www.asciiart.eu/image-to-ascii
  Input:    openrfs-mark-flat.png
  Settings: Characters 32, ASCII gradient "Black and White"
            (blockelement), dithering None, and every other control at
            its default - brightness/contrast/saturation 100%, hue 0,
            grayscale/sepia/invert 0, thresholding, sharpness and edge
            detection all unticked, space density 1, transparent frame 0
  Taken:    18 September 2026

It was run in a browser driven by Playwright, so it can be redone: set
those controls, upload that file, copy the output, trim the blank rows
top and bottom.

WHY THAT TOOL AND NOT A RAMP HERE. This file used to reduce the drawing
itself, classifying each cell into one of five things. It drew a fish,
and next to the converter's it was a poor one - it cannot render an edge
that runs diagonally through a cell, which is most of a drawing's edges.
Three of the converter's character sets were tried at this size and two
of them read as noise in a 8x16 bitmap font: a tonal gradient needs a
denser grid than a terminal has. The block set does not, because it
draws a SHAPE and leaves the tone to the colour.

The width is 32 rather than the 46 it was first taken at. 46 is 360
pixels of an 80-column terminal and looks less like a fetch than like a
poster; 26 was tried too and is too small for the mark to keep its eyes.
32 gives twelve rows, which is also two more than the fetch has facts -
though gfetch no longer depends on that, since running to the mark's
height alone dropped a fact the moment the mark got shorter than the
list.

The colours are still computed in tools/make-logo.py, because the
converter emits text and has none to give. The mark is classified on the
same grid the art is laid out on, each cell takes whichever of paper,
outline, body, tongue and eye holds most of it, and each class is inked
in the mean of every source pixel that fell into it. So the fish is
drawn in the fish's own reds rather than in anything anybody picked.

TWO THINGS THAT LOOKED WRONG AND WHY.

The drawing is built out of black lines - the outside edge, the mouth,
the fins, the rims of the eyes - and a cell here is thirty-odd pixels of
the mark, so every one of those lines is thinner than the cell it lands
in. Inked black on a black terminal each one is a bite out of the fish.
Keeping only the lines that bound something was tried and so was keeping
only the ones that make a pupil; the first leaves a mass of black where
this drawing's lines are thickest, which is around the eyes. So the line
is not drawn at all, except where it borders the white of an eye, where
it is a pupil. The fish is red and the terminal is black: a silhouette
does not have to draw its own edge.

And brightness alone is not enough to be the tongue. The antialiased rim
of a black pupil against a white eye is a grey as light as the tongue
is, so the only two cells that came out "tongue" were both inside an eye
and the tongue itself was never drawn. The tongue is PINK - its red
channel runs well clear of its other two, and a grey's does not - so
that is what is asked. At 32 columns the tongue is two cells, because at
32 columns the tongue is two cells wide; more of it needs a wider mark.
